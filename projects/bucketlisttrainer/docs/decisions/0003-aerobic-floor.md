# ADR-0003: Aerobic work never drops below the block floor

**Status:** accepted · 2026-07-28

## Context
The natural instinct in a power block or a max strength block is to cut cardio entirely.
The interference literature (Hickson 1980; Wilson et al. 2012) supports the instinct:
concurrent endurance work does measurably blunt strength and power adaptation, in
proportion to its dose.

## Decision
Never fewer than 2 aerobic sessions per week, rising to 3 during Max Strength and 4
during the two summer blocks. Enforced by the `aerobic-floor` invariant.

## Rationale
The interference cost is real but bounded. The cost of losing the base is not. Aerobic
adaptations take months to build and cannot be recovered in a spring, and two of the
twelve goals (Gallatin Crest, Bangtail) plus one more (the mile, roughly 85% aerobic)
depend entirely on it.

Trading a small amount of strength adaptation for the Crest is the correct trade, made
deliberately rather than by accident.

## Mitigation
The maintenance dose runs on the **bike**, not running. Wilson's meta-analysis found
cycling interferes substantially less than running. This is the cheapest available
concession and it doubles as Bangtail preparation.

## Exemption
Event weeks are exempt. A taper is supposed to cut volume.
